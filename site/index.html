    How a block walks out

    A whale who sells 3% of supply into the pool bites every holder at once. A block goes around the pool: the seller and one buyer swap directly, at a price they both agreed to.

    1

    The seller lists

    Token, size, price in SOL, and an optional lock. The wallet signs a message. Nothing moves.

    2

    A buyer asks

    Buyers ask with a signed message. The seller sees every wallet that asked, or names one up front.

    3

    The seller signs for one buyer

    The swap is signed against a durable nonce, not a recent blockhash, so the signature waits for days instead of a minute.

    4

    The buyer walks out

    The buyer adds a signature and sends. Tokens and SOL change hands in one transaction, or nothing happens at all.

    Why there is always a named buyer

    A discounted block open to anyone is a free meal for a bot: it would take it and sell into the pool in the same second. Each block here is signed for exactly one wallet.

    A better price for holding

    The seller can ask the buyer to hold. The tokens then land in a Jupiter Lock escrow in the buyer's name until a date, and nobody can cancel it, the seller included.

    What PLOVER never touches

    No escrow of ours, no program of ours. The swap is plain transfers, the lock is Jupiter Lock, and your browser builds every transaction.

    Backing out

    Until the buyer sends it, the seller can close the nonce. The signed block can then never land, and the nonce rent comes back.

    Sell a block

    Your tokens stay in your wallet until a buyer takes the block. If you sell them somewhere else first, the block just fails.

    Listing is free. Signing for a buyer opens a nonce account (about 0.00145 SOL, back to you when it closes). A lock adds about 0.003 SOL of escrow rent.
